# The Chronicle of Ostley Crossing

> Provenance: the player compiled this chronicle from the notes of six sessions played with a human Game Master. It is the text the player hands over to continue the campaign alone. This copy is invented for the Threshold test suite: every person, place and group in it is fictional.

## The player character

Teodor Brandt is thirty-four years old. Two winters ago he took over the ferry at Ostley Crossing from his late uncle, and he has worked it every day since. He is careful with money, slow to trust, and he keeps his word once he gives it.

Teodor has a second name. Among the Lamplighters he is called "Wick". Agathe Morrow and Sefa Lind know that Wick is Teodor. Odile Quast, the reeve, does not know it, and neither does Gerrit Vane. Nobody at the Bargemen's Guild knows it.

## How we play

The Game Master plays the world and every other character. The player decides what Teodor does, says and feels. Scenes are slow and grounded: weather, money, work and small debts matter more than fights. When an action is uncertain, the Game Master asks for a roll and the dice decide. The story is told in English.

## The places

Ostley Crossing is a village of forty houses on the south bank of the river Sallow, around the ferry landing and the ferry house where Teodor lives.

The Copper Kettle is the inn of Ostley Crossing, three streets up from the landing. Its common room has the only big hearth in the village, and the Lamplighters leave letters behind its bar.

Harrowmere is the market town half a day upriver. The Bargemen's Guild has its hall there, and the river toll is paid at its stone quay.

## The groups

The Bargemen's Guild owns most of the barges on the Sallow and sets the river toll. Last spring the Guild raised the toll to four pennies a crossing, and the villagers still talk about it.

The Lamplighters are a quiet network of couriers who carry letters past the Guild's tolls and past the reeve's seals. They have no hall. They meet where they can and they pass letters hand to hand.

## The people of the crossing

Agathe Morrow is sixty-seven. She has run the Copper Kettle for thirty years and she is the oldest Lamplighter on this stretch of the river. She brought Teodor into the network last summer. Her knees are bad, and she wants to sell the inn before the next winter, though she has told no one but her sister in Harrowmere.

Sefa Lind is a courier for the Lamplighters. The text does not give her age; she looks barely grown. She walks the towpath between Harrowmere and Ostley Crossing twice a week, and she sleeps in the Copper Kettle's loft when the weather is bad. She never asks what is in the letters she carries, and she does not like people who do.

Odile Quast is forty-five and has been the reeve of Ostley Crossing for three years. She was elected after the old reeve drowned. She is honest and stubborn, and she believes the ferry should belong to the village, not to one man. She keeps the village chest and the toll strongbox in her own house, and she writes every penny in a book with a green cover.

Pim Quast is Odile's son. He is eleven years old. He works the ferry rope for Teodor after school for a penny a day, and he counts every penny twice.

## People with their own business

Gerrit Vane is the master of the Guild hall at Harrowmere. The bargemen call him the Toll-Master. His age is not given. He fined the barge captain Ludo Brenn twenty pennies last month for a late cargo of salt.

Ludo Brenn is a barge captain of the Guild. His age is not given. He drinks at the Copper Kettle when his barge waits for the tide, and he talks too much when he drinks.

Henrike Sall is a potter in Ostley Crossing. Her age is not given. Her life has little to do with the ferry: her kiln cracked in the first frost of the winter, she owes the Guild for a load of clay, and her only son works on a farm far to the south. She has met Teodor perhaps three times, always to pay a crossing.

Osric Fell is fifty-two and keeps the toll ledger at the Harrowmere quay. He has never been to Ostley Crossing that anyone remembers, and he has never met Teodor. His mind is on two things: his daughter's wedding and a boundary wall that his neighbour moved one foot onto his land. He has saved for the wedding feast for six years, in a tin box under his bed, and he has already argued twice with the cook about the price of the pies. He took the boundary wall to the Harrowmere magistrate in the winter and lost, and he has not forgiven his neighbour.

Henrike works alone. She sells bowls and jugs at the Harrowmere market and to the Copper Kettle, and she sings while she throws pots, loud enough to be heard from the street.

## What has happened

These events are settled. They happened in play and they stay true.

1. In the autumn flood the old ferry rope snapped. Teodor replaced it with an iron chain that he bought from a smith in Harrowmere, and the ferry ran again after four days.
2. Ten days ago Sefa Lind carried a sealed letter from Harrowmere to the Copper Kettle. Agathe gave it to Teodor. Teodor has not opened it. He hid it under the floor of the ferry house.
3. The toll money of the last market day, eighty pennies, went missing between the ferry landing and the reeve's strongbox. Odile Quast counted the box herself and found it short.
4. Henrike Sall asked the reeve for a loan to repair her kiln. Odile refused, because the village chest is nearly empty after the flood.
5. Gerrit Vane came down to the crossing once, in the autumn, and looked at the ferry for a long time without crossing.
6. In the fifth session Ludo Brenn, drunk at the Copper Kettle, told the whole common room that the Guild "always gets what it wants on this river". Agathe threw him out. He came back the next week and paid for the broken mug.
7. In the sixth session Teodor carried Odile and Pim across in the fog without taking the toll. Odile thanked him and said that it changed nothing about the ferry.

## What is planned, proposed or uncertain

None of the items in this part has happened yet. They are planned, proposed, pending or not established.

- The Bargemen's Guild has proposed to buy the ferry from Teodor for sixty silver marks. Teodor has not answered. The offer is still open.
- Osric Fell's daughter's wedding is planned for the first day of spring at Harrowmere. It has not happened yet.
- A meeting of the Lamplighters is planned at the Copper Kettle for the next new moon. Agathe chose the date; nobody has told Sefa yet.
- The reeve's inquiry into the missing toll money is pending. Odile says she will question everyone who handled the box, Teodor included, after the market day.
- Who wrote the sealed letter is not established. The seal is a plain wax seal with no mark.
- Henrike Sall has a buyer for her potter's wheel in Harrowmere. The sale has not happened yet: the buyer will come on the next market day if the river is calm.
- Agathe has proposed that Teodor carry the Lamplighters' winter post himself, upriver to Harrowmere. Teodor has not agreed.

## Pause here

The chronicle stops on the evening of the third day of the thaw, in the common room of the Copper Kettle. Teodor has just come in from the last crossing of the day, wet to the knees. Agathe Morrow is behind the bar. Sefa Lind sits in the corner with her boots by the fire. Pim Quast is asleep on the bench by the hearth, because his mother is at Harrowmere for the night. Nobody has spoken yet.
